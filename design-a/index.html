Pounamu is given, not bought for yourself

That's the custom many New Zealanders still keep. A piece of greenstone carries the goodwill of the person who gives it, and it's said to gather mana the longer it's worn. Plenty are handed down through families.

So most of what we sell leaves as a gift. Every piece arrives wrapped, with a card that explains its form and what it stands for.

Buying for yourself? Plenty of people do. Ask someone you love to hand it over.

Every form carries a meaning

Carvers shape pounamu into a handful of traditional forms. Choose one to see it.

Koru

The unfurling fern frond

New life, growth and fresh starts. The koru is the young shoot of the silver fern, curled tight before it opens. It's a common gift for a birth, a new home or a new chapter.

The stall has closed. The collection has moved here, and every piece is still chosen by hand.
